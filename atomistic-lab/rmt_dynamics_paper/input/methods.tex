\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Construction of the Correlation Matrix}
\label{subsec:matrix-computation}
From $M$ velocity snapshots at intervals $\delta t$ we estimate Equation~\ref{eqn:Cij} as
\begin{align}
    \hat{C}_{ij} = \frac{\delta t}{6} \sum\limits_{\tau = -L}^{L} \left(1 - \frac{|\tau|}{L}\right) \frac{1}{M} \sum\limits_{m} \mathbf{v}_{i}(t_{m} + \tau\, \delta t) \cdot \mathbf{v}_{j}(t_{m}),
    \label{eqn:Chat}
\end{align}
with $L = \tau_{\max}/\delta t$,
where the factor $1/6$ combines the component average of Equation~\ref{eqn:Cij} with the factor $1/2$ that converts the two-sided sum to a one-sided integral, and the sum over $m$ runs over time origins with zero padding outside the trajectory.
The two-sided sum is tapered to zero at the lag $\tau_{\max}$ by a triangular (Bartlett) window; unlike a sharp truncation, this makes $\hat{C}$ positive semi-definite by construction, as the entropy of Equation~\ref{eqn:vnentropy} requires.
The cross-correlations are evaluated in frequency space, at a cost of $\mathcal{O}(|\N|^{2} M \log M)$.
The lag must be long enough to integrate the correlations of interest and short enough not to accumulate noise from the tail; by default we set $\tau_{\max}$ to about ten times the integral relaxation time $\tau_{\text{int}}$ of the velocity autocorrelation, and Section~\ref{subsec:bond-addition} shows how the choice sets which bonds can be resolved.

\subsection{Marchenko--Pastur Null}
\label{subsec:de-noising-correlation-matrices}
For $|\N|$ independent particles and $T_{\text{eff}}$ independent samples, the spectrum of $\hat{C}$ follows the Marchenko--Pastur density~\cite{marchenko1967}
\begin{align}
    \rho_{\text{MP}}(\mu) = \frac{1}{2 \pi q\, s^{2}\, \mu} \sqrt{(\mu_{+} - \mu)(\mu - \mu_{-})},
    \label{eqn:mp-density}
\end{align}
on $\mu_{-} \le \mu \le \mu_{+}$ with edges $\mu_{\pm} = s^{2} (1 \pm \sqrt{q})^{2}$, aspect ratio $q = |\N| / T_{\text{eff}}$ and noise scale $s^{2}$, which we take from the mean diagonal element of a reference state (the bond-free liquid in Section~\ref{subsec:bond-addition}, the run itself elsewhere).
$T_{\text{eff}}$ is a property of the estimator, not of the decorrelation time alone.
Equation~\ref{eqn:Chat} is a quadratic form $\hat{C} = V^{\top} A V$ in the $M \times |\N|$ matrix of samples, with $A$ the banded matrix of lag weights; for independent particles with temporal covariance $R$, matching its first two spectral moments to those of a Wishart matrix gives
\begin{align}
    T_{\text{eff}} = d\, \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(A R)^{2}}{\mathrm{Tr}(A R A R)} \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{3 d}{2} \frac{T_{\text{traj}}}{\tau_{\max}},
    \label{eqn:teff}
\end{align}
with $d = 3$ components, the limit holding for $\tau_{\max} \gg \tau_{\text{int}}$.
Lengthening the lag thus reduces the sample count in proportion, and the naive count $T_{\text{traj}}/\tau_{\text{int}}$ overstates it by a factor of order $\tau_{\max}/\tau_{\text{int}}$.
Matching two moments is an approximation to the exact limiting spectrum of sample covariance matrices with temporally correlated or weighted samples~\cite{burda2005,pafka2004}; Section~\ref{subsec:convergence-of-the-noise-term} shows that it reproduces the bulk to within a Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance of $0.03$ for $q \le 0.3$.
We evaluate the traces in the frequency domain from the lag weights and the measured particle-averaged velocity power spectrum, and keep $q$ well below one.

The null is used in two ways.
Eigenvalues below $\mu_{-}$ or above $\mu_{+}$ are counted as leaving the bulk.
For the entropy, because noise alone lowers $S$ by an amount that grows with $q$, we report the entropy deficit
\begin{align}
    \Delta S = S_{\text{MP}}(q) - S(\hat{C}),
    \label{eqn:entropy-deficit}
\end{align}
where $S_{\text{MP}}(q)$ is the mean entropy of a white Wishart matrix of the same size and aspect ratio, averaged over $16$ realisations (standard error below $3 \times 10^{-5}$ in $S/\ln|\N|$ for $q \le 0.3$); $\Delta S$ vanishes for a spectrum indistinguishable from noise and is positive when correlation is concentrated in fewer modes.
As a check on both the Gaussian assumption and the sample count, we also build a surrogate null from each trajectory by shifting every particle's time series circularly by an independent random offset, which keeps its autocorrelation and distribution but removes cross-correlations; Section~\ref{subsec:convergence-of-the-noise-term} validates both.

\subsection{Molecular Dynamics Simulations}
\label{subsec:molecular-dynamics-simulations}
All simulations use LAMMPS~\cite{thompson2022} with Nos\'e--Hoover thermostats and barostats (coupling times $0.1$~ps and $1$~ps), so that total momentum is conserved and distinct correlations are not damped by a stochastic bath.
Every state is simulated with three independent seeds, and error bars are the standard deviation over seeds.
Where positions rather than velocities are recorded, $C$ is built from the mean velocity over each sampling interval, $[\mathbf{r}_{i}(t + \delta t) - \mathbf{r}_{i}(t)]/\delta t$ from unwrapped coordinates, which has the same time integral as the instantaneous velocity without aliasing fast motion, so the sampling interval can be chosen for storage.

\paragraph{Lennard-Jones systems.}
Argon parameters are used ($\sigma = 3.405$~\AA{}, $\varepsilon/k_{B} = 119.8$~K, $m = 39.948$~amu, cutoff $3\sigma$, $\delta t = 2$~fs).
For bond addition, an $NVT$ liquid at $80$~K and $0.8\,\sigma^{-3}$ is equilibrated, harmonic bonds ($k_{b} = 100\,\varepsilon/\sigma^{2}$, rest length equal to the current separation) are added between random eligible pairs, and every bond count, including zero, restarts from the same configuration with $10^{4}$ steps of re-thermalisation.
The short-lag protocol uses $|\N| = 512$, pairs closer than $2\sigma$ and $1$~ns of production with velocities every $20$~fs; the long-lag protocol uses $|\N| = 216$, pairs closer than $1.3\sigma$ and $26$~ns with positions every $0.5$~ps.
The phase sweep uses $|\N| = 500$ along the $20$~atm isobar, which lies below the critical pressure but, unlike the $1$~atm isobar, has a liquid range of tens of kelvin; each temperature from $20$ to $160$~K is run from an fcc crystal, a dense disordered state ($0.8\,\sigma^{-3}$) and a dilute gas ($0.02\,\sigma^{-3}$) to expose superheating and supercooling, with $0.3$~ns of $NPT$ equilibration and $1$~ns of production.

\paragraph{Water.}
TIP4P/2005~\cite{abascal2005} with $432$ rigid molecules (SHAKE), a $10$~\AA{} cutoff with long-range dispersion correction, particle-particle particle-mesh electrostatics and $\delta t = 2$~fs is followed along the $1$~atm isobar from $200$ to $340$~K.
Each temperature is run from a proton-disordered ice Ih crystal ($6 \times 3 \times 3$ orthorhombic cells) and from a liquid melted at $400$~K, with $0.5$~ns of equilibration and $3$~ns of production, positions every $0.1$~ps.
The ice cell is relaxed anisotropically, except at $320$ and $340$~K, where the crystal melts during equilibration and the anisotropic cell becomes unstable, and the liquid isotropically.
The ice configuration satisfies the Bernal--Fowler rules~\cite{bernal1933}: the hydrogen-bond network is oriented along a random Euler circuit of the four-coordinated oxygen lattice, which gives every oxygen two donated and two accepted bonds, and the net dipole is zero.

\paragraph{Molten salt.}
NaCl is modelled with the Tosi--Fumi rigid-ion potential~\cite{fumi1964,tosi1964} with $256$ ion pairs, an $11$~\AA{} cutoff, particle-particle particle-mesh electrostatics and $\delta t = 1$~fs, along the $1$~atm isobar from $900$ to $1800$~K.
Each temperature is run from a rock-salt crystal and from a liquid melted at $2000$~K, with $0.1$~ns of equilibration and $1.5$~ns of production, positions every $50$~fs.

Input templates, drivers, analysis code and the seeds of every run are available as described in Section~\ref{sec:data-and-availability}.
