\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Construction of the Correlation Matrix}
\label{subsec:matrix-computation}
Given a molecular dynamics trajectory of length $T_{\text{traj}}$ from which we sample $M$ velocity snapshots at uniform intervals $\delta t$, we compute the finite-sample estimator of Equation~\ref{eqn:Cij} as
\begin{align}
    \hat{C}_{ij} = \frac{\delta t}{2} \sum\limits_{\tau = -L}^{L} \left(1 - \frac{|\tau|}{L}\right) \frac{1}{M} \sum\limits_{m} \mathbf{v}_{i}(t_{m} + \tau\, \delta t) \cdot \mathbf{v}_{j}(t_{m}), \qquad L = \tau_{\max} / \delta t,
    \label{eqn:Chat}
\end{align}
where the inner sum averages over all time origins $t_{m}$ for which both snapshots lie inside the trajectory, and the outer sum is the symmetrised (two-sided) form of the time integral, tapered to zero at a lag $\tau_{\max}$ that we choose to be several times the integral relaxation time of the velocity autocorrelation (see below).
The triangular (Bartlett) taper is not a cosmetic choice: a sharp truncation at $\tau_{\max}$ does not yield a positive semi-definite matrix, whereas the Bartlett-weighted estimator is positive semi-definite by construction, which the entropy of Equation~\ref{eqn:vnentropy} requires.

Evaluating Equation~\ref{eqn:Chat} by direct summation costs $\mathcal{O}(|\N|^{2} M^{2})$ and is intractable at the system sizes we use.
Instead, for each pair $(i,j)$ we compute the cross-correlation in frequency space via the Wiener--Khinchin theorem, using a single pair of FFTs per Cartesian component and summing the three component contributions, reducing the cost to $\mathcal{O}(|\N|^{2} M \log M)$.
The implementation is memory-bound at large $|\N|$, so we process blocks of pair indices in sequence and reuse the forward transforms of each atom's velocity time series.

Two practical points deserve attention.
First, when $a_{i}$ is a vector such as velocity, we compute $\hat{C}_{ij}$ per Cartesian component and average; this reduces variance by a factor of three relative to using a scalar projection and preserves rotational invariance.
Second, the choice of $\tau_{\max}$ is not innocuous.
Too short a cutoff under-integrates long-lived correlations and biases $\hat{C}$ downward; too long a cutoff accumulates noise from the flat tail of the autocorrelation function and inflates the variance of every off-diagonal entry.
We estimate the integral relaxation time
\begin{align}
    \tau_{\text{int}} = \int\limits_{0}^{\infty} dt \, \frac{\langle \mathbf{v}_{i}(t) \cdot \mathbf{v}_{i}(0) \rangle}{\langle \mathbf{v}_{i}(0) \cdot \mathbf{v}_{i}(0) \rangle}
    \label{eqn:tau-int}
\end{align}
directly from the trajectory, averaging over atoms, and set $\tau_{\max} = 10\, \tau_{\text{int}}$ by default in diffusive systems; where $\tau_{\text{int}}$ vanishes because the motion is bound, $\tau_{\max}$ is fixed by hand.

\subsection{De-noising via the Marchenko--Pastur Null}
\label{subsec:de-noising-correlation-matrices}
For a system of $|\N|$ non-interacting particles sampled at $T_{\text{eff}}$ independent times, the bulk of the eigenvalue distribution of the trace-normalised correlation matrix $\hat{C} / \mathrm{Tr}(\hat{C})$ follows the Marchenko--Pastur density~\cite{marchenko1967},
\begin{align}
    \rho_{\text{MP}}(\mu) = \frac{1}{2 \pi q\, \sigma^{2}\, \mu} \sqrt{(\mu_{+} - \mu)(\mu - \mu_{-})}, \qquad \mu \in [\mu_{-}, \mu_{+}],
    \label{eqn:mp-density}
\end{align}
with aspect ratio $q = |\N| / T_{\text{eff}}$, edges $\mu_{\pm} = \sigma^{2}\, (1 \pm \sqrt{q})^{2}$, and noise scale $\sigma^{2}$ fitted from the bulk.
The sample count $T_{\text{eff}}$ is a property of the estimator and not of the decorrelation time alone.
Equation~\ref{eqn:Chat} is a quadratic form $\hat{C} = V^{\top} A V$ in the $M \times |\N|$ matrix of velocity samples, with $A$ the banded matrix of lag weights; for independent particles with temporal covariance $R$, matching its first two spectral moments to those of a Wishart matrix gives
\begin{align}
    T_{\text{eff}} = d\, \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(A R)^{2}}{\mathrm{Tr}(A R A R)} \;\longrightarrow\; \frac{3 d}{2} \frac{T_{\text{traj}}}{\tau_{\max}} \qquad (\tau_{\max} \gg \tau_{\text{int}}),
    \label{eqn:teff}
\end{align}
where $d = 3$ is the number of Cartesian components averaged over.
Lengthening $\tau_{\max}$ therefore reduces the number of independent samples in proportion, and the count $T_{\text{traj}} / \tau_{\text{int}}$ overstates it by a factor of order $\tau_{\max} / \tau_{\text{int}}$.
We evaluate the traces in Equation~\ref{eqn:teff} in the frequency domain from the lag weights and the measured particle-averaged velocity power spectrum, and keep $q$ well below one, where the Marchenko--Pastur form is an accurate description of the weighted estimator.
We refer to $\rho_{\text{MP}}$ as the noise-only null and use it in two ways.

First, we test how close the empirical spectrum $\rho_{\text{emp}}(\mu)$ is to the null using the Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance $D_{\mathrm{KS}}(\rho_{\text{emp}}, \rho_{\text{MP}})$ and a comparison between the empirical largest eigenvalue $\mu_{\max}$ and the MP edge $\mu_{+}$.
For a system with genuine correlation structure, $D_{\mathrm{KS}}$ remains non-trivial even in the $T_{\text{eff}} \to \infty$ limit and $\mu_{\max}$ sits above $\mu_{+}$; for an uncorrelated reference, both quantities approach zero.

Second, we use the null to separate signal from noise in the entropy observable.
Eigenvalues above a threshold $\mu_{\text{cut}} = \mu_{+} (1 + \kappa / \sqrt{T_{\text{eff}}})$, with $\kappa$ a small safety factor set to $\kappa = 3$ throughout, are classified as outliers; the remainder of the spectrum is taken to be consistent with the null.
We report two entropy quantities: the raw $S(\hat{C})$ of Equation~\ref{eqn:vnentropy} and the de-noised version
\begin{align}
    S_{\text{signal}}(\hat{C}) = S(\hat{C}) - S_{\text{bulk}}(\hat{C}),
    \label{eqn:signal-entropy}
\end{align}
where $S_{\text{bulk}}$ is the von Neumann entropy computed from only those eigenvalues below $\mu_{\text{cut}}$.
$S_{\text{signal}}$ is the component of the spectrum that cannot be explained by the noise-only null and is the quantity most sensitive to the physical features we want to expose.

We validate this machinery on synthetic trajectories without correlation structure before applying it to molecular dynamics output; the validation is reported in Section~\ref{subsec:convergence-of-the-noise-term}.

\subsection{Molecular Dynamics Simulations}
\label{subsec:molecular-dynamics-simulations}
Simulations fall into three families, one per experimental system, and use the classical molecular dynamics code LAMMPS~\cite{thompson2022} unless otherwise noted.
Production runs of interacting systems use a Nos\'e--Hoover thermostat with a coupling time of $0.1$~ps in the constant-$NVT$ ensemble, and a Nos\'e--Hoover barostat with coupling constants of $0.1$~ps (thermostat) and $1.0$~ps (barostat) where $NPT$ is required, so that the dynamics conserve total momentum and the distinct correlations are not damped by a stochastic bath.
The validation of the noise null in Section~\ref{subsec:convergence-of-the-noise-term} uses synthetic Ornstein--Uhlenbeck velocities instead of a simulation.
Each run is initialised from a randomly seeded velocity distribution and separately reported in output metadata, and every reported observable is averaged over a minimum of three and up to five independent seeds.

\paragraph{Lennard-Jones systems (Sections~\ref{subsec:bond-addition} and~\ref{subsec:lennard-jones-phases}).}
The Lennard-Jones potential is taken with argon parameters $\sigma = 3.405$~\AA{}, $\varepsilon / k_{B} = 119.8$~K, and particle mass $39.948$~amu, truncated at $r_{c} = 3\,\sigma$.
System size is $|\N| = 500$ for the phase sweep and $|\N| = 512$ or $216$ for the two bond-addition protocols.
The integration timestep is $\delta t = 2$~fs.
For the phase sweep we equilibrate for $1.5 \times 10^{5}$ steps in $NPT$ and run for $5 \times 10^{5}$ steps of production along the $P = 20$~atm isobar, which lies below the critical pressure and, unlike the $1$~atm isobar, has a liquid range of several tens of kelvin.
Because a periodic system of this size superheats and supercools, every temperature is simulated from three initial conditions --- an fcc crystal, a dense disordered configuration at $\rho = 0.8\,\sigma^{-3}$, and a dilute gas at $\rho = 0.02\,\sigma^{-3}$ --- so that each transition is bracketed from both sides;
for the bond-addition experiment we equilibrate an $NVT$ liquid at $T = 80$~K and $\rho = 0.8\,\sigma^{-3}$, insert bonds into the equilibrated configuration, re-thermalise for $10^{4}$ steps, and run for $5 \times 10^{5}$ production steps, with the bond-free reference restarted from the same configuration; a second set of bond-addition runs uses $|\N| = 216$, bonds within $1.3\,\sigma$ only, and $1.3 \times 10^{7}$ production steps with positions written every $250$ steps.
Harmonic bonds for the bond-addition experiment are added between random eligible pairs with stiffness $k_{b} = 100\,\varepsilon/\sigma^{2}$ and rest length set at the current pair separation.
Bonds are inserted by writing a bonded data file from the equilibrated configuration and restarting from it.

\paragraph{Water (Section~\ref{subsec:water-phases}).}
Water is modelled with the rigid TIP4P/2005 model~\cite{abascal2005}, held rigid by SHAKE, with $432$ molecules ($1296$ atoms), a $10$~\AA{} real-space cutoff for both the Lennard-Jones and Coulomb terms with a long-range dispersion correction, and particle-particle particle-mesh electrostatics.
The integration timestep is $\delta t = 2$~fs.
Along the $1$~atm isobar, every temperature in $\{200, 220, 240, 260, 270, 280, 290, 300, 310, 320, 340\}$~K is simulated from two initial states with three seeds each: a proton-disordered ice Ih crystal of $6 \times 3 \times 3$ orthorhombic cells, relaxed with an anisotropic barostat, and a liquid melted at $400$~K for $50$~ps before cooling, relaxed isotropically.
The ice configuration is generated to satisfy the Bernal--Fowler rules~\cite{bernal1933} by orienting the hydrogen-bond network along a random Euler circuit of the four-coordinated oxygen lattice, which assigns every oxygen exactly two donated and two accepted bonds, and has zero net dipole.
Each run is equilibrated for $0.5$~ns and followed for $3$~ns, with positions written every $0.1$~ps.

\paragraph{Molten salt (Section~\ref{subsec:molten-salt-ionic-conductivity}).}
NaCl is modelled with the Tosi--Fumi rigid-ion potential~\cite{fumi1964,tosi1964} with $256$ ion pairs, a $11$~\AA{} real-space cutoff and particle-particle particle-mesh electrostatics, in the constant-$NPT$ ensemble at $1$~atm with $\delta t = 1$~fs.
Every temperature in $\{900, 1000, 1100, 1200, 1300, 1400, 1600, 1800\}$~K is simulated from a rock-salt crystal and from a liquid melted at $2000$~K for $20$~ps, with three seeds each; runs are equilibrated for $100$~ps and followed for $1.5$~ns, with positions written every $50$~fs.

\paragraph{Correlation matrices from positions.}
Where positions rather than velocities are recorded (the long-lag bond-addition runs, water and the salt), $C$ is built from the mean velocity over each sampling interval, $[\mathbf{r}_{i}(t + \delta t) - \mathbf{r}_{i}(t)]/\delta t$, using unwrapped coordinates.
This has the same time integral as the instantaneous velocity and does not alias fast motion into the zero-frequency estimate, so the sampling interval can be chosen for storage rather than for the fastest vibration.
