\subsection{Validation of the Noise Null}
\label{subsec:convergence-of-the-noise-term}
Before turning to interacting systems we check the null of Section~\ref{subsec:de-noising-correlation-matrices} on a system with no correlation structure at all: $|\N| = 256$ independent Ornstein--Uhlenbeck velocity processes with a correlation time of $5$ sampling intervals, $2 \times 10^{4}$ samples long, for which every off-diagonal element of $C$ is sampling noise.
With the lag-window sample count of Equation~\ref{eqn:teff}, the Marchenko--Pastur density describes the spectrum at every lag from $2$ to $20$ correlation times (Figure~\ref{fig:noise-null}): the Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance stays between $0.011$ and $0.029$, the largest eigenvalue lies within $1\%$ of the upper edge, and no more than one of the $256$ eigenvalues falls outside the bulk.
The naive count $T_{\text{traj}}/\tau_{\text{int}}$ ignores the lag and fails increasingly as the lag grows: at $\tau_{\max} = 10\,\tau_{\text{int}}$ it places $30$ eigenvalues above and $40$ below a bulk that is too narrow, with a Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance of $0.18$, and at $20\,\tau_{\text{int}}$ the distance reaches $0.29$.
Every count of outliers in what follows uses the lag-window sample count.

\subsection{Bond Addition}
\label{subsec:bond-addition}
To test how bonding enters the spectrum under controlled conditions, we add $k$ harmonic bonds to an equilibrated Lennard-Jones liquid at $T = 80$~K and $\rho = 0.8\,\sigma^{-3}$ and compare against the same liquid restarted without bonds (Figure~\ref{fig:concept}).
Two protocols are used.
In the first, $|\N| = 512$, bonds join random pairs closer than $2\sigma$, each run is $1$~ns long and $C$ is evaluated with $\tau_{\max} = 2$~ps ($T_{\text{eff}} \approx 2260$, $q \approx 0.23$).
In the second, chosen to satisfy the lag condition of Section~\ref{subsec:interpreting-atomic-correlation-matrices}, $|\N| = 216$, bonds are restricted to the first coordination shell ($r_{0} < 1.3\,\sigma$), each run is $26$~ns long and $\tau_{\max} = 100$~ps ($T_{\text{eff}} \approx 1170$, $q \approx 0.18$).
All quantities are averaged over three seeds; Figure~\ref{fig:bonding} collects the results.

(a, b) Eigenvalue density in units of the mean diagonal element of unbonded atoms, for increasing bond counts (light to dark); the dashed curve and shaded band are the Marchenko--Pastur density and support of the bond-free liquid. (c) Relative-motion mode $(C_{ii}+C_{jj}-2C_{ij})/2$ of bonded and unbonded pairs against the integration lag, for one $16$-bond trajectory, with the lower bulk edge where it is defined ($q < 1$); the blue point is the $100$~ps protocol. (d) Eigenvalues below the bulk of the bond-free liquid, excluding the momentum zero mode, against the number of independent bond constraints. (f) Decrease of the von Neumann entropy relative to the bond-free liquid.}
    \label{fig:bonding}
\end{figure}

At $\tau_{\max} = 2$~ps the bond-free spectrum is bulk-like (Figure~\ref{fig:bonding}a): a single eigenvalue sits at zero, as required by conservation of total momentum, and $11$ to $12$ of the $512$ eigenvalues lie above the upper edge.
These upper modes are delocalised, with participation ratios near $170$, and are present before any bond is added, so they are not a bonding signature.
Adding bonds produces no new localised modes above the bulk; instead, weight moves towards and through the lower edge, as anticipated in Section~\ref{subsec:interpreting-atomic-correlation-matrices}.

The effect of a bond is unambiguous at the level of a single pair.
The relative-motion mode of a bonded pair is $0.61 \pm 0.01$ of the free value at $k = 64$ and $0.52 \pm 0.01$ at $k = 256$, against $0.99 \pm 0.04$ for unbonded pairs.
This reduction is not large enough to carry the mode out of the bulk, whose lower edge lies at $0.28$ in the same units, so the number of sub-bulk eigenvalues lags far behind the number of constraints (Figure~\ref{fig:bonding}d and Table~\ref{tab:bond-addition}): it is zero at $k = 16$, reaches $53$ of $254$ at $k = 256$, and approaches the constraint count only at $k = 1024$, where the bond network spans almost the whole system and diffusion is largely arrested.
The lowest eigenvectors nonetheless carry the bond topology before individual modes separate, capturing $18\%$ and $37\%$ of the constraint space at $k = 16$ and $64$, six and three times the value for random modes (Figure~\ref{fig:bonding}e).

The incomplete separation at short lags is a consequence of the lag, not of the estimator.
A bond fixes the separation of a pair but not its orientation, so its relative coordinate is distinguishable from free diffusion only when $\tau_{\max} \gg r_{0}^{2}/6D$; with $D \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-4}$~\AA$^{2}$\,fs$^{-1}$ and $r_{0}$ up to $2\sigma$, this requires tens of picoseconds.
Re-evaluating one $16$-bond trajectory with longer lags confirms the trend (Figure~\ref{fig:bonding}c): the bonded mode falls from $0.61$ at $2$~ps to $0.39$ at $40$~ps while unbonded pairs stay at $0.98$--$0.99$, but beyond $10$~ps a $1$~ns trajectory has too few independent samples for the bulk edge to be defined.

The second protocol meets both conditions, and the bond signature becomes spectral as well as pair-wise (Figure~\ref{fig:bonding}b).
The bonded relative-motion mode falls to $0.13$--$0.15$ of the free value, well below the lower bulk edge at $0.33$.
In all three seeds exactly $8$ and $27$ eigenvalues leave the bulk through its lower edge for $8$ and $27$ bonds, and $51$--$52$ do so for $54$ bonds; at $k = 108$, where bonded atoms make up most of the system and the diffusive baseline shifts, the count is $91$--$95$ for $106$--$108$ constraints (Figure~\ref{fig:bonding}d).
The corresponding eigenvectors reproduce the bond graph, capturing $86$--$94\%$ of the constraint space against $4$--$25\%$ for random modes (Figure~\ref{fig:bonding}e); in the example of Figure~\ref{fig:concept}, $98\%$ of the weight of the $27$ bond modes lies on the $51$ bonded atoms, a quarter of the system.
The bond-free liquid has a single eigenvalue above the upper edge in this regime, compared with $11$ to $12$ at $2$~ps.

In both protocols the von Neumann entropy decreases monotonically with the number of bonds (Figure~\ref{fig:bonding}f), from $6.1133 \pm 0.0005$ to $5.697 \pm 0.007$ at $\tau_{\max} = 2$~ps and from $5.2791 \pm 0.0005$ to $5.0448 \pm 0.0013$ at $100$~ps; at $2$~ps the $16$-bond system is separated from the bond-free liquid by about three times the seed scatter.
Bonding is therefore read off the spectrum of $C$ as a count of sub-bulk eigenvalues, one per constraint, and off its eigenvectors as the bonding topology, provided the lag exceeds the time a free pair needs to diffuse over the bond length; at shorter lags the same information is present pair by pair but does not separate from the noise bulk.

\subsection{Lennard-Jones Phases}
\label{subsec:lennard-jones-phases}
We next follow $C$ ($\tau_{\max} = 2$~ps) across the solid, liquid and vapour phases of the Lennard-Jones system along the $20$~atm isobar, from three starting states that expose the hysteresis of the first-order transitions in a $500$-atom box (Section~\ref{subsec:molecular-dynamics-simulations}).
Because $q$ changes by an order of magnitude along the isobar, the raw entropy mixes structure with sampling noise (in the liquid $S/\ln|\N|$ falls from $0.9805$ to $0.9758$ between $80$ and $130$~K largely because $q$ rises from $0.21$ to $0.30$), so we report the entropy deficit of Equation~\ref{eqn:entropy-deficit}, omitting the vapour, where $q > 1$ and the null is not reliable.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_lj_phases.pdf}
    \caption{Lennard-Jones system along the $20$~atm isobar from a crystal (blue circles), a dense disordered state (orange squares) and a dilute gas (aqua triangles), three seeds per point. (a) Mass density. (b) Entropy deficit of $C$ relative to the Marchenko--Pastur null at the same aspect ratio, shown where $q < 0.6$. (c) Mean diagonal element of $C$, proportional to the self-diffusion coefficient. (d--f) Pooled eigenvalue densities in units of the mean eigenvalue for the crystal and the disordered solid at $40$~K and the liquid at $100$~K, against the Marchenko--Pastur density at each run's $q$ (dashed; shaded band is its support).}
    \label{fig:lj-results}
\end{figure}

The crystal melts between $90$ and $95$~K (Figure~\ref{fig:lj-results}a); the disordered starts are liquid from $60$~K upwards and solidify between $50$ and $60$~K into a solid less dense than the crystal, whose structure (amorphous or polycrystalline) we did not determine.
The liquid vaporises between $130$ and $135$~K on heating, while the dilute start condenses only between $110$ and $115$~K, so melting coexistence lies between $50$ and $95$~K and liquid--vapour coexistence between $110$ and $135$~K.

The entropy deficit separates the three condensed states.
In the crystal $\Delta S/\ln|\N|$ rises from $0.040$ at $20$~K to $0.064$ at $90$~K; in the disordered solid it is larger, $0.062$ to $0.074$; and in the liquid it is an order of magnitude smaller, $0.0097$ at $60$~K, decaying monotonically to $10^{-4}$ at $120$~K and to zero within the seed scatter of a few $10^{-5}$ above.
With a seed scatter of $10^{-4}$ or less, this decay is resolved at every temperature, and the crystalline and disordered solids differ by a factor of about $1.5$ at the same temperature and similar density.

Every step in $\Delta S$ coincides with a step in density and diffusion at the same grid point on every branch.
On the $5$~K grid both steps complete within one interval (the full widths at half maximum of $|d\rho/dT|$ and $|dS/dT|$ are $10$--$11$~K, set by the spacing), so these data do not show that $S(C)$ resolves a transition more sharply than the density.
The spectra (Figure~\ref{fig:lj-results}d--f) show the difference between phases directly: the liquid follows the Marchenko--Pastur density closely, whereas both solids pile weight into small eigenvalues below the bulk and spread a tail of large ones beyond it.

The physical content of $\Delta S$ differs between phases.
In the solids the diagonal of $C$ is two orders of magnitude smaller than in the liquid (Figure~\ref{fig:lj-results}c), and since $C$ vanishes for a non-diffusing solid at long lags, its structure at $2$~ps reflects vibrational correlations that have not decayed within the window.
In the liquid it reflects correlated displacements of neighbouring atoms, which weaken with temperature until the spectrum is indistinguishable from noise.
$\Delta S$ is not a function of diffusion alone: it falls as diffusion rises at melting and within the liquid, but rises with diffusion along the solid branches.

\subsection{Water Phases}
\label{subsec:water-phases}
Water adds rigid molecular structure and a hydrogen-bond network to the questions above.
We follow TIP4P/2005 water along the $1$~atm isobar from $200$ to $340$~K, from a proton-disordered ice Ih crystal and from a pre-melted liquid, so that melting near the model's melting point of $252$~K is bracketed by superheated ice and supercooled liquid in the same way as for the Lennard-Jones crystal.
$C$ is built for the oxygen atoms alone and for all atoms, which separates the motion of molecular centres from the intramolecular correlation of each oxygen with its two hydrogens.
Results are collected in Figure~\ref{fig:water}.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig_water.pdf}
    \caption{TIP4P/2005 water along the $1$~atm isobar from ice Ih (blue circles) and from a pre-melted liquid (orange squares), three seeds per point. (a) Density. (b) Mean diagonal element of the oxygen-only matrix $C_{\mathrm{O}}$, proportional to the self-diffusion coefficient. (c) Entropy deficit of $C_{\mathrm{O}}$ relative to the Marchenko--Pastur null. (d) Eigenvalues of the all-atom matrix for the liquid at $300$~K, in units of the mean of the $432$ largest, pooled over seeds; the dashed line separates the $864$ smallest. (e) Fraction of the intramolecular relative-coordinate space (two O--H vectors per molecule) captured by the $864$ lowest eigenvectors of the all-atom matrix, with the value for random modes. (f) Spearman correlation between the amplitude of each oxygen in the five leading eigenvectors of $C_{\mathrm{O}}$ and its time-averaged hydrogen-bond count.}
    \label{fig:water}
\end{figure}

The ice remains crystalline up to $310$~K, its density falling slowly from $0.928$ to $0.910$~g\,cm$^{-3}$, and melts between $310$ and $320$~K, where the seeds split and the diffusion coefficient rises fourteenfold (Figure~\ref{fig:water}a,b).
The liquid branch never freezes on the time scale of the runs.
It passes through a density maximum of $1.0015$~g\,cm$^{-3}$ between $270$ and $280$~K, close to the maximum of the model near $278$~K, and below $240$~K becomes increasingly sluggish, its diffusion coefficient at $200$~K being thirty times smaller than at $340$~K and comparable to the finite-window residual of the ice.
As for the Lennard-Jones crystal and the salt, superheating is large, and melting of this model at $252$~K is bracketed only from above.

The all-atom matrix tests the bonding result of Section~\ref{subsec:bond-addition} on molecules whose geometry is held fixed.
Each rigid molecule has two bounded relative coordinates, the vectors from its oxygen to its two hydrogens, so $C$ should have $2 \times 432 = 864$ eigenvalues far below the remaining $432$, which describe the diffusion of the molecules.
It does (Figure~\ref{fig:water}d): the spectrum splits into three bands of $432$ eigenvalues, two of them at $0.9$--$1.9\%$ and $4.8$--$6.9\%$ of the mean of the molecular band, and the $864$ lowest eigenvectors capture $97.8$--$99.6\%$ of the intramolecular relative-coordinate space at every temperature and in both phases, against $67\%$ for random modes (Figure~\ref{fig:water}e).
The lag condition is met here by a wide margin: with O--H vectors of $0.96$~\AA{} and $D \approx 2.6 \times 10^{-4}$~\AA$^{2}$\,fs$^{-1}$ at $300$~K, $r_{0}^{2}/6D \approx 0.6$~ps against $\tau_{\max} = 4$~ps.
We have not resolved which relative motions make up the two intramolecular bands; their separation by a factor of about four suggests orientational relaxation at different rates about different molecular axes.

The oxygen-only matrix follows the molecular centres.
Its entropy deficit (Figure~\ref{fig:water}c) is large in the ice, $0.087$--$0.119$, and in the liquid it is small and nearly constant above $260$~K ($0.0028$--$0.0053$), but it grows steeply on supercooling, to $0.011$ at $240$~K, $0.038$ at $220$~K and $0.074$ at $200$~K, approaching the values of the ice as the liquid's diffusion approaches that of the crystal.
The deficit thus tracks the slowing of the liquid continuously, with no transition at which to compare it against the density; at the lowest temperatures, as in the solids, it reflects motion that has not decorrelated within the window.

Finally, the leading eigenvectors of $C_{\mathrm{O}}$ are related to the hydrogen-bond network, but not in the sense we had expected.
In the ice, where nearly every molecule has four hydrogen bonds, the amplitude of an oxygen in the five leading eigenvectors is uncorrelated with its bond count.
In the liquid above $260$~K the two are anticorrelated, with a Spearman coefficient falling from $-0.18$ at $260$~K to $-0.43$ at $340$~K ($p < 10^{-3}$ for every temperature from $270$~K upwards) as the mean bond count falls from $3.73$ to $3.02$: the leading collective modes are carried preferentially by molecules with fewer hydrogen bonds, not by the most strongly bonded ones.

\subsection{Molten Salt Ionic Conductivity}
\label{subsec:molten-salt-ionic-conductivity}
Molten NaCl tests the transport decomposition of Section~\ref{subsec:microscopic-origins-of-transport-properties} directly.
With velocities as the single-particle primitive and the ionic charges as $\eta_{\alpha}$, the Nernst--Einstein deviation $\Delta^{\sigma}$ of the ionic conductivity is a ratio of block sums of $C$, and must agree with the deviation obtained from the collective charge current.
We follow the Tosi--Fumi model along the $1$~atm isobar from $900$ to $1800$~K, from a rock-salt crystal and from a pre-melted liquid, which brackets melting near $1070$~K and gives the conductivity of the liquid over a wide temperature range.
Results are collected in Figure~\ref{fig:salt}.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{figures/fig_salt.pdf}
    \caption{Tosi--Fumi NaCl along the $1$~atm isobar, three seeds per point. (a) Density from the crystal (blue circles) and liquid (orange squares) starts. (b) Ionic conductivity from the charge current and its Nernst--Einstein estimate, for every melted state. (c) Nernst--Einstein deviation $\Delta^{\sigma}$ from the species-block sums of $C$ and from the time-domain Green--Kubo integral of the charge current (offset by $12$~K for clarity). (d) Entropy deficit of $C$ relative to the Marchenko--Pastur null.}
    \label{fig:salt}
\end{figure}

As for the Lennard-Jones crystal, the hysteresis is wide.
The rock-salt crystal persists to $1200$~K and has melted by $1300$~K, where its density drops from $1.815$ to $1.36$~g\,cm$^{-3}$ and the ionic self-diffusion coefficients rise by a factor of about $40$; the pre-melted liquid remains liquid down to $900$~K.
Above $1300$~K the two branches coincide.

The conductivity of the liquid is $302 \pm 3$~S\,m$^{-1}$ at $900$~K and $357 \pm 15$~S\,m$^{-1}$ at $1100$~K, rising to a maximum of about $400$~S\,m$^{-1}$ near $1600$~K and falling slightly at $1800$~K, while its Nernst--Einstein estimate rises monotonically from $316$ to $645$~S\,m$^{-1}$.
The deviation between the two is the quantity that Equation~\ref{eqn:full-correction} expresses through the species blocks of $C$.
With $\eta_{\mathrm{Na}} = +1$ and $\eta_{\mathrm{Cl}} = -1$, the block-sum route gives $\Delta^{\sigma} = -0.045 \pm 0.012$ at $900$~K, falling to $-0.14$ at $1100$~K and $-0.42$ at $1800$~K, and agrees within the seed scatter with the deviation obtained from the time-domain Green--Kubo integral of the charge current at every temperature (Figure~\ref{fig:salt}c); the two estimates of the charge-current integral differ by $1$--$6\%$, the size expected from their different treatment of the integration window.
Distinct cation--anion correlation therefore reduces the conductivity below the uncorrelated estimate by an amount that grows with temperature along the isobar, as the liquid expands and the Na--Cl coordination number within $3.6$~\AA{} falls from $4.2$ at $900$~K to $2.5$ at $1800$~K.
In the crystal both routes are dominated by the residual of the finite window and do not measure a conductivity; we do not report them.

The spectrum of $C$ registers the same trend.
The entropy deficit of the liquid is small, $0.0018$ at $900$~K, and grows monotonically to $0.0126$ at $1800$~K (Figure~\ref{fig:salt}d), in the opposite direction to the Lennard-Jones liquid, whose deficit decays with temperature.
Across all $36$ melted runs the deficit and $\Delta^{\sigma}$ are strongly anticorrelated (Spearman $\rho = -0.95$): the more of the motion is carried by correlated cation--anion pairs, the more the spectrum departs from the noise null.
Both quantities change monotonically with temperature, so this correlation partly reflects a common trend rather than an independent test; it nonetheless shows that the departure of the spectrum from the null follows the distinct correlations that set the transport coefficient.
The crystal again carries a large deficit, $0.030$--$0.037$, an order of magnitude above the liquid, and loses it on melting.
