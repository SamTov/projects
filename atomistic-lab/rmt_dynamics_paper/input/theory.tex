\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Green--Kubo Relations}
\label{subsec:onsager-relations}
Close to equilibrium, thermodynamic forces $X_{j}$ drive conjugate fluxes through Onsager's linear relations $\langle J_{i} \rangle = \sum_{j} L_{ij} X_{j}$, with $L_{ij} = L_{ji}$ by microscopic reversibility~\cite{onsager31a,onsager31b,callen51a}.
Green and Kubo~\cite{green54a,kubo57a,kubo66a} expressed each coefficient as the time integral of an equilibrium flux autocorrelation,
\begin{align}
    L_{ij} = \frac{1}{k_{B}T V} \int\limits_{0}^{\infty} dt \, \langle J_{i}(t) \cdot J_{j}(0) \rangle,
    \label{eqn:green-kubo-general}
\end{align}
with $V$ the volume and $T$ the temperature.
This is the relation we decompose into particle-wise contributions.

\subsection{Microscopic Decomposition of Transport Coefficients}
\label{subsec:microscopic-origins-of-transport-properties}
Decompositions of a transport coefficient into a self term and a correction from distinct pair correlations are well established for individual properties, most prominently the ionic conductivity of electrolytes and molten salts~\cite{kashyap11a,fong20a,fong21a}.
We write the same decomposition once, for any coefficient derived from a single-particle primitive; the notation is collected in Table~\ref{tab:definitions}.

Consider a coefficient $\lambda$ given by
\begin{align}
    \lambda = \frac{\beta}{3} \int\limits_{0}^{\infty} dt \, \langle A(t)\cdot A(0) \rangle,
    \qquad
    A(t) = \sum\limits_{\alpha \in \Se} \eta_{\alpha} \sum\limits_{i \in \N_{\alpha}} a_{i}(t),
    \label{eqn:genericsystem}
\end{align}
with $\beta = 1/k_{B}T$, where $a_{i}$ is a property of atom $i$ (its velocity, for the coefficients considered here) and $\eta_{\alpha}$ a scalar attached to its species, such as a charge or a mass.
Grouping the pairs $(i, j)$ in the double sum by species, and separating $i = j$ from $i \neq j$, gives
\begin{align}
    \lambda = \frac{\beta}{3} \sum\limits_{\alpha, \gamma \in \Se} \eta_{\alpha} \eta_{\gamma} \left( \delta_{\alpha\gamma} f_{\alpha} + g_{\alpha\gamma} \right),
    \label{eqn:expansion}
\end{align}
with the self and distinct integrals
\begin{align}
    f_{\alpha} = \int\limits_{0}^{\infty} dt \sum\limits_{i \in \N_{\alpha}} \langle a_{i}(t)\cdot a_{i}(0) \rangle, \qquad
    g_{\alpha \gamma} = \int\limits_{0}^{\infty} dt \sum\limits_{i \in \N_{\alpha}} \sum\limits_{\substack{j \in \N_{\gamma} \\ j \neq i}} \langle a_{i}(t)\cdot a_{j}(0) \rangle.
    \label{eqn:fgdefs}
\end{align}
The distinct terms enter as a dimensionless correction to the Nernst--Einstein estimate $\lambda^{\text{NE}}$ built from the self terms alone,
\begin{align}
    \lambda = \lambda^{\text{NE}} \left(1 + \Delta^{\lambda}\right), \qquad
    \Delta^{\lambda} = \frac{\sum_{\alpha, \gamma} \eta_{\alpha}\, \eta_{\gamma}\, g_{\alpha \gamma}}
                           {\sum_{\alpha} \eta_{\alpha}^{2}\, f_{\alpha}},
    \label{eqn:full-correction}
\end{align}
so that $\Delta^{\lambda}$ measures the departure of the coefficient from its uncorrelated single-particle baseline.
For a species-resolved collective property such as the electrophoretic mobility the same steps give $\Delta^{\lambda}_{\alpha} = \sum_{\gamma} \eta_{\gamma}\, g_{\alpha\gamma} / (\eta_{\alpha} f_{\alpha})$.
Coefficients whose flux contains an explicit pair-interaction term, such as the shear viscosity and thermal conductivity, require an atom-wise partition of that term~\cite{irving1950} before the decomposition applies; we restrict the experiments to coefficients of the form of Equation~\ref{eqn:genericsystem}.

\subsection{The Atom-Wise Correlation Matrix}
\label{subsec:atom-wise-correlation-matrix}
The integrals of Equation~\ref{eqn:fgdefs} are sums over atoms; promoting the atom indices to matrix indices defines the \emph{atom-wise correlation matrix}
\begin{align}
    C_{ij} = \int\limits_{0}^{\infty} dt \, \langle a_{i}(t) \cdot a_{j}(0) \rangle, \qquad i, j \in \N.
    \label{eqn:Cij}
\end{align}
By time-reversal symmetry $C$ is real and symmetric.
Ordering atoms by species partitions it into blocks $C^{(\alpha\gamma)}$, and $f_{\alpha}$ and $g_{\alpha\gamma}$ are the trace and the off-diagonal sum of these blocks, so every coefficient of the form of Equation~\ref{eqn:expansion} is a linear functional of $C$.
The spectrum of $C$ contains more than these functionals report.

In practice $C$ is estimated from a finite trajectory, and its entries carry sampling noise set by an effective number of independent samples $T_{\text{eff}}$, which depends on the trajectory length relative to the integration lag $\tau_{\max}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:de-noising-correlation-matrices}).
For independent particles the off-diagonal entries are inner products of independent signals and the spectrum follows a Marchenko--Pastur distribution~\cite{marchenko1967} set by $|\N|$ and $T_{\text{eff}}$.
This uncorrelated limit is the null against which physical structure is measured.

\subsection{Spectral Observables}
\label{subsec:interpreting-atomic-correlation-matrices}
Because the integrated autocorrelation of any linear combination $\sum_{i} x_{i} a_{i}$ is non-negative in equilibrium~\cite{hansen90a}, $C$ is positive semi-definite, with eigenvalues $\mu_{k} \geq 0$ and orthonormal eigenvectors $\phi_{k}$.

\paragraph{Spectrum.}
Eigenvalues inside the Marchenko--Pastur bulk are consistent with noise; those outside it carry physical signal, and their eigenvectors identify the atoms involved.
Which edge they leave through follows from the Einstein form of Equation~\ref{eqn:Cij},
\begin{align}
    C_{ij} = \lim_{t \to \infty} \frac{1}{2t} \langle \Delta \mathbf{r}_{i}(t) \cdot \Delta \mathbf{r}_{j}(t) \rangle,
    \label{eqn:Cij-einstein}
\end{align}
which identifies $C$ as the matrix of long-time displacement covariances, so that any coordinate whose excursions stay bounded is a null direction.
A bond between atoms $i$ and $j$ bounds their relative coordinate, and the antisymmetric combination $(\phi_{i}, \phi_{j}) \propto (1, -1)$ is driven to zero eigenvalue while the symmetric one carries the diffusion of the pair.
Bonding therefore removes eigenvalues through the \emph{lower} edge of the bulk, one per independent constraint.
With a finite lag two conditions apply: a bond must survive for longer than $\tau_{\max}$ to register, and because a bond of length $r_{0}$ fixes the separation but not the orientation of the pair, the relative coordinate is distinguishable from free diffusion only once $\tau_{\max} \gg r_{0}^{2}/6D$.
Eigenvalues above the upper edge instead signal collective transport beyond the single-particle baseline.
The same argument implies that $C$ vanishes in a non-diffusing crystal, whose measured spectrum reflects only the finite-lag residual of the estimator.
The participation ratio
\begin{align}
    \mathrm{PR}_{k} = \Big( \sum\nolimits_{i} \phi_{ik}^{4} \Big)^{-1}
    \label{eqn:participation-ratio}
\end{align}
counts the atoms active in mode $k$, from about $2$ for an isolated bonded pair to order $|\N|$ for a collective mode.
When many bonds are present their null modes are nearly degenerate and mix, so the subspace spanned by the sub-bulk modes, rather than any single eigenvector, is the object to compare with the bonding topology.

\paragraph{Entropy.}
The von Neumann entropy
\begin{align}
    S(C) = -\sum\limits_{k} p_{k} \ln p_{k}, \qquad p_{k} = \frac{\mu_{k}}{\sum_{l} \mu_{l}},
    \label{eqn:vnentropy}
\end{align}
is the entropy of the trace-normalised operator $C/\mathrm{Tr}\,C$, in parallel with the entropy of a density matrix and with Schlitter's entropy of the positional covariance~\cite{schlitter1993}.
It is maximal, $\ln |\N|$, for a uniform spectrum and falls as correlation concentrates in fewer modes.
Because sampling noise alone spreads the spectrum by an amount set by $T_{\text{eff}}$, we report it relative to the noise null (Section~\ref{subsec:de-noising-correlation-matrices}).
